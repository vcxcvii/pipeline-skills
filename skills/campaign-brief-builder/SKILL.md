# Campaign Brief Builder (B2B SaaS)

A campaign is one argument delivered across the places a buyer already goes. A
calendar of activities that ship on the same date is not a campaign, and it is
the most common reason activity rises while pipeline does not.

## Setup

Ask:

1. What is the commercial bet? A launch, a new segment, a competitive
   displacement, a pricing change, or a reactivation.
2. What number should move, and from what to what, by when?
3. Which audience, and what do they currently believe?
4. What is already built that we can reuse?

If the answer to (2) is "awareness", stop and get a pipeline number. A campaign
with no target number cannot be judged, so it will be judged on how it looked.

## Build the brief

### 1. The one argument

A single sentence the whole campaign carries. Every asset makes this argument
more specific, never a different one. Test: read the sentence to someone in
sales. If they say "sure, but what does that mean for my deals", it is not
specific enough yet.

### 2. The belief shift

What the audience believes today, what you need them to believe by the end, and
the evidence that moves them. Name the evidence. Campaigns without evidence
become repetition, and repetition without proof reads as noise.

### 3. The asset set, in order of dependency

List assets in build order, and mark each with the argument stage it serves.
Cut anything that does not advance the argument, however cheap it is to make.
Typical spine: one anchor asset, one landing page, one sequence, three social
posts, one enablement note.

### 4. The sales handoff

What sales receives, when, and what they are expected to do with it. Most
campaigns die here: demand arrives and nobody told the people who answer it.
Specify the trigger, the owner, and the response time.

### 5. The measurement plan

The single number, the leading indicator that predicts it, the measurement
window, and who reads the result. Also write the kill condition: what result
would make you stop, and when you will check.

## Output

- The campaign argument, one sentence
- The belief shift, with the evidence
- The asset list in build order, with owners
- The sales handoff, with trigger and response time
- The measurement plan, including the kill condition
- What this campaign will deliberately not do

## Rules

- One argument. If the brief contains two, it is two campaigns, and you should
  say so.
- Never plan a channel you cannot staff for the whole window.
- Reuse before you build. Note what already exists.
- No em-dashes, no "integrated 360 campaign", no vanity metrics as the target.
