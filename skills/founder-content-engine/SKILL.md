# Founder Content Engine (B2B SaaS)

Founder content fails for one of two reasons: the founder has no time, or the
content has no opinion. This skill fixes the second one and then designs around
the first.

## Setup

Interview the founder for the raw material. Ask these, and keep the answers
verbatim:

1. What does almost everyone in your category believe that you think is wrong?
2. What did you believe two years ago that you no longer believe, and what
   changed your mind?
3. What do buyers consistently get wrong in the first call?
4. What is the most expensive mistake you watched a customer make?
5. What are you refusing to build, and why?

If the answers are safe and agreeable, push once: ask what they would say if no
prospect were watching. Safe answers produce content nobody remembers.

## Build the engine

### 1. Isolate the spikes

From the answers, pull three to five positions that are specific enough to
disagree with. Discard anything that a competitor's founder could post
unchanged. That test removes most of the list, and that is the point.

### 2. Turn each spike into a post ladder

For every position, draft three posts at different depths: the claim on its own,
the story that proves it, and the tactical how-to that follows from it. One
position becomes three weeks of material without repeating itself.

### 3. Write to the shape of the platform

Open with the claim, not the setup. One idea per post. Short lines. No hook
formulas, no "here's what I learned" preamble, no engagement bait. If a post
needs a listicle to be interesting, the idea underneath is weak.

### 4. Design the 90-minute rhythm

One 45-minute recorded conversation every two weeks produces the raw material.
The rest is editing. Specify who edits, who posts, and what the founder actually
has to do, which should be talking and approving, never drafting from blank.

### 5. Decide what compounds

Pick the one asset per month that outlives the feed: a teardown, a data point, a
written position. Feed posts point at it. Without this, the engine produces
attention that ends when the scrolling ends.

## Output

- The three to five positions, written as claims
- Nine to fifteen drafted posts, grouped by position
- The two-week operating rhythm, with owners
- The monthly compounding asset, named and scoped
- A kill list: the generic topics this founder should never post about

## Rules

- Keep the founder's own words and speech rhythm. If the draft sounds like a
  marketer wrote it, it will not get posted, and it should not be.
- Never fabricate a story, a customer, or a number from the founder's life.
- No em-dashes, no engagement bait, no "unpopular opinion" framing.
- If the founder has no genuine disagreement with their category, say so. The
  honest fix is research, not a posting schedule.
